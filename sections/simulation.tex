\subsection{Experiments}
Setup, \emph{total} and PoF as in Section~\ref{sec:setup}. We report both outcomes throughout. Experiments A and B use a strict penalty, $\lambda=2$; Experiment C varies it.

\textbf{(A) Acceptance sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:parity-accept}).}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Saved:} acceptance barely matters. As $\mu_L$ falls from $0.8$ to $0.15$, total greedy's total only falls from $0.71$ to $0.68$, and no method's DP gap exceeds $0.12$. DP greedy's PoF stays between $1.06$ and $1.11$, far below the formula at low $\mu_L$ ($1.91$ at $\mu_L=0.15$), which does not model saved nodes.
\item \emph{Shielding} is the likely reason. At $\mu_L=0.3$, total greedy (saved run) places no seeds in $L$, yet $0.60$ of $L$'s exposed nodes are saved and only $0.08$ converted.
\item \emph{Converted:} acceptance matters a lot. At $\mu_L=0.3$, total greedy again places no seeds in $L$ and leaves a gap of $0.35$ ($r_H=0.43$, $r_L=0.08$). Its gap is still $0.15$ with equal target means ($\mu_L=0.8$). We did not separate how much of this comes from $L$ being smaller and sparser and how much from its slightly lower realized mean ($0.78$ vs.\ $0.81$).
\item DP greedy reaches near-parity at $\mu_L=0.3$ (gap $0.011$), but its total falls from $0.37$ to $0.15$ (PoF $2.47$), above the formula's $1.35$. $\varepsilon$-DP greedy meets $\varepsilon=0.05$ there (gap $0.040$, total $0.16$).
\item These DP seeds also block far less of the rumor. On the saved outcome their total is $0.26$, against $0.68$ for total greedy's converted-run seeds.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{(B) Budget sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:parity-budget}), $\mu_L=0.3$, converted.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Claim 2's plateau does not appear} up to $k=30$. DP greedy's total keeps rising ($0.15$ at $k=10$, $0.28$ at $k=20$, $0.36$ at $k=30$) while its gap stays below $0.02$. Each $L$ seed counts itself and spills truth into $H$, which the ceiling model leaves out. Whether a plateau appears at larger budgets, or if greedy may stop early ($|S_P|<k$), remains open.
\item \emph{The bound holds, through the seeds.} $L$'s rate passes its mean acceptance ($0.28$) at $k=21$. At $k=30$ all seeds are in $L$ and account for $76\%$ of its rate ($0.37$); its non-seed rate is $0.09$.
\item \emph{Strict parity levels down.} At $k=10$, DP greedy lowers $H$'s rate from $0.43$ to $0.15$. $L$'s rate rises from $0.08$ to $0.14$, but its non-seed rate is lower than under total greedy ($0.05$ vs.\ $0.08$), so the rise comes from counting the seeds.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{(C) Trade-off and seed placement (Fig.~\ref{fig:parity-pareto}), $k=10$, $\mu_L=0.3$.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Saved:} raising $\lambda$ trades total for gap, from $0.69$ with a gap of $0.11$ ($\lambda=0$) to $0.58$ with a gap of $0.03$ ($\lambda=8$), though not monotonically.
\item \emph{Converted:} seeds move into $L$ gradually as $\lambda$ grows: $0$, $4$, $6$, $8$, $9$ and $10$ of $10$ at $\lambda=0.25$, $0.5$, $0.6$, $0.7$, $0.75$ and $0.85$. All-$L$ first appears just past $w_H\approx0.81$, where $H$'s rate takes a negative weight. Over this range the total falls from $0.37$ to $0.19$ and the gap from $0.35$ to $0.03$. Beyond $\lambda=1$ there is no clear further trend.
\item \emph{All-$L$ comes from the objective.} At $\lambda=2$, the best $H$ seed lowers $F_\lambda$ at every step (first step: $r_H$ $+0.060$, $r_L$ $+0.011$, $F_\lambda$ $-0.046$), while the best $L$ seed raises it ($+0.022$, $+0.018$, $+0.013$). Forcing $0$ to $10$ seeds into $L$, $F_\lambda$ rises with each added $L$ seed, from $-0.27$ to $0.15$, so greedy's order is an unlikely cause, though each split is still built greedily. Excluding seeds from the rates still gives $10$ of $10$.
\item \emph{Claim 1's allocation is not what the greedy methods choose.} It gives $4$ of $10$ seeds in $L$, and that split leaves a gap of $0.26$ (PoF $1.10$). One reason is that seeds differ more than $a_H/a_L\approx2.9$ suggests: at $\lambda=2$ the best first $H$ seed converts about $11$ nodes of $H$, while the best first $L$ seed converts under $1$ node of $L$, counting itself, and about $4$ of $H$, which also breaks the model's within-group assumption. This fits direction 1's point that a resistant group also forwards the truth less.
\item \emph{Seeding $L$ does not persuade more of $L$.} $L$'s non-seed rate falls from $0.075$ with no $L$ seeds to $0.051$ with $10$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Limitations.} These tests are preliminary. They use one small synthetic network of $500$ nodes and do not cover large or real graphs, other homophily levels, or other settings of $\sigma$, $p$ and the rumor seeds. We report no error bars, and DP greedy's PoF is not even monotone in $\mu_L$ ($2.47$ at $0.3$, $2.40$ at $0.15$). We varied $\lambda$ only at $k=10$, $\mu_L=0.3$; we expect looser values to soften the cost in A and B as well, but have not tested this.

\textbf{Takeaway.} Under unequal acceptance, what is measured decides the conclusion. Parity on saved nodes is cheap. Strict parity on converted nodes is costly and is reached mainly by holding back the receptive group, while looser penalties trade total for gap gradually.
