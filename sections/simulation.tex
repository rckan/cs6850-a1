\textbf{(A) Acceptance sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:parity-accept}).}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Saved:} acceptance barely matters. As $\mu_L$ falls from $0.8$ to $0.15$, total greedy's total only falls from $0.71$ to $0.68$, and no method's DP gap exceeds $0.12$. DP greedy's PoF stays between $1.06$ and $1.11$, far below the formula at low $\mu_L$ ($1.91$ at $\mu_L=0.15$), which does not model saved nodes.
\item \emph{Shielding:} this is the likely reason. At $\mu_L=0.3$, total greedy (saved run) places no seeds in $L$, yet $0.60$ of $L$'s exposed nodes are saved and only $0.08$ converted.
\item \emph{Converted:} acceptance matters a lot. At $\mu_L=0.3$, total greedy again places no seeds in $L$ and leaves a gap of $0.35$ ($r_H=0.43$, $r_L=0.08$). Even with equal means ($\mu_L=0.8$) its gap is $0.15$, so network position also disadvantages $L$, which is smaller and sparser. \todo{Confirm this reading: equal means is only one run, and $L$'s lower realized mean ($0.78$ vs.\ $0.81$) also contributes.}
\item DP greedy reaches near-parity at $\mu_L=0.3$ (gap $0.011$), but its total falls from $0.37$ to $0.15$ (PoF $2.47$). $\varepsilon$-DP greedy meets $\varepsilon=0.05$ there (gap $0.040$, total $0.16$).
\item These DP seeds also block far less of the rumor. Measured on the saved outcome, their total is about $0.26$, against $0.68$ for total greedy's converted-run seeds.
\item \todo{Compare with Matthew's maximin PoF and $\mathbb{M}$ at the same settings, after both experiments are rerun on one machine. The current figures in the two sections do not match.}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{(B) Budget sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:parity-budget}), $\mu_L=0.3$, converted.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Claim 2's plateau does not appear.} DP greedy's total keeps rising through $k=30$ ($0.15$ at $k=10$, $0.28$ at $k=20$, $0.36$ at $k=30$), and its gap stays below $0.02$ for $k\ge5$.
\item \emph{The bound holds, through the seeds.} $L$'s rate passes its mean acceptance ($0.28$) at $k=21$, which the bound allows only through the seeds' own share. At $k=30$ all seeds are in $L$, and they make up $76\%$ of $L$'s rate ($0.37$); its non-seed rate is $0.09$.
\item \emph{DP's parity is leveling down, not better persuasion.} At $k=10$, DP greedy lowers $H$'s rate from $0.43$ to $0.15$, while $L$'s non-seed rate is lower than under total greedy ($0.05$ vs.\ $0.08$). $L$'s overall rate rises from $0.08$ to $0.14$ only because the seeds count.
\item \emph{Claim 1's allocation does not match DP greedy.} The ceiling model predicts $k_g\propto E_g/\bar a_g$, about $4$ of $10$ seeds in $L$; DP greedy places all $10$ there for every $\mu_L\le0.6$. With no seeds in $H$, $H$'s rate of $0.15$ comes entirely from spillover. \todo{Interpretation unconfirmed: is spillover from $L$ into $H$ why DP avoids $H$ entirely, or is greedy order responsible? Try brute force on a small graph, or compare with a forced $E_g/\bar a_g$ split.}

\end{itemize}

\textbf{(C) Trade-off (Fig.~\ref{fig:parity-pareto}), $k=10$, $\mu_L=0.3$.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Saved:} raising $\lambda$ trades total gradually, from $0.69$ with a gap of $0.11$ ($\lambda=0$) to $0.58$ with a gap of $0.03$ ($\lambda=8$).
\item \emph{Converted:} the change is abrupt. The gap falls from $0.26$ at $\lambda=0.5$ to $0.007$ at $\lambda=1$, and total from $0.34$ to $0.16$; larger $\lambda$ adds nothing beyond noise.
\item A possible explanation: when $r_H\ge r_L$, $F_\lambda=(w_H-\lambda)r_H+(w_L+\lambda)r_L$, so $H$'s rate gets a negative weight once $\lambda>w_H$, and here $w_H\approx0.82$. \todo{Check this algebra, and test the threshold with $\lambda$ values near $0.82$; the sweep has none between $0.5$ and $1$.}
\end{itemize}
