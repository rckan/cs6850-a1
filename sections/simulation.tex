\subsection{Experiments}\label{sec:parity-exp}
Setup, \emph{total} and PoF as in Section~\ref{sec:setup}. We report both outcomes throughout. Experiments A and B use a strict penalty, $\lambda=2$; Experiment C varies it.

\textbf{(A) Acceptance sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:parity-accept}).}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Saved:} acceptance barely matters. As $\mu_L$ falls from $0.8$ to $0.15$, total greedy's total only falls from $0.71$ to $0.68$, and no method's DP gap exceeds $0.12$. DP greedy's PoF stays between $1.06$ and $1.11$, far below the formula at low $\mu_L$ ($1.91$ at $\mu_L=0.15$), which does not model saved nodes.
\item \emph{Shielding} is the likely reason. At $\mu_L=0.3$, total greedy (saved run) places no seeds in $L$, yet $0.60$ of $L$'s exposed nodes are saved and only $0.08$ converted.
\item \emph{Converted:} acceptance matters a lot. At $\mu_L=0.3$, total greedy again places no seeds in $L$ and leaves a gap of $0.35$ ($r_H=0.43$, $r_L=0.08$). Its gap is still $0.15$ with equal target means ($\mu_L=0.8$). We did not separate how much of this comes from $L$ being smaller and sparser and how much from its slightly lower realized mean ($0.78$ vs.\ $0.81$).
\item DP greedy reaches near-parity at $\mu_L=0.3$ (gap $0.011$), but its total falls from $0.37$ to $0.15$ (PoF $2.47$). The ceiling formula gives $1.35$ here, but its assumptions fail in this setup (C), so we do not treat it as a benchmark. $\varepsilon$-DP greedy meets $\varepsilon=0.05$ there (gap $0.040$, total $0.16$).
\item These DP seeds also block far less of the rumor. On the saved outcome their total is $0.26$, against $0.68$ for total greedy's converted-run seeds.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{(B) Budget sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:parity-budget}), $\mu_L=0.3$, converted.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Claim 2's plateau does not appear} up to $k=30$. DP greedy's total keeps rising ($0.15$ at $k=10$, $0.28$ at $k=20$, $0.36$ at $k=30$) while its gap stays below $0.02$. $L$ never saturates in the model's sense: at $k=30$ all seeds are in $L$, its non-seed rate is $0.09$, far below $\bar a_L=0.28$, and the seeds make up $76\%$ of its rate ($0.37$). Its overall rate passes $0.28$ at $k=21$ only through the seeds, as the bound allows. Each $L$ seed also spills truth into $H$. Whether a plateau appears at larger budgets, or if greedy may stop early ($|S_P|<k$), remains open.
\item \emph{Strict parity levels down, well before saturation.} At $k=10$, DP greedy lowers $H$'s rate from $0.43$ to $0.15$ through seed choice, a mechanism outside the ceiling model (Claim 2). $L$'s rate rises from $0.08$ to $0.14$, but its non-seed rate is lower than under total greedy ($0.05$ vs.\ $0.08$), so the rise comes from counting the seeds.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{(C) Trade-off and seed placement (Fig.~\ref{fig:parity-pareto}), $k=10$, $\mu_L=0.3$.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Saved:} raising $\lambda$ trades total for gap, from $0.69$ with a gap of $0.11$ ($\lambda=0$) to $0.58$ with a gap of $0.03$ ($\lambda=8$), though not monotonically.
\item \emph{Converted:} seeds move into $L$ gradually as $\lambda$ grows: $0$, $4$, $6$, $8$, $9$ and $10$ of $10$ at $\lambda=0.25$, $0.5$, $0.6$, $0.7$, $0.75$ and $0.85$. Over this range the total falls from $0.37$ to $0.19$ and the gap from $0.35$ to $0.03$. Beyond $\lambda=1$ there is no clear further trend.
\item \emph{Why seeds move to $L$.} An $H$ seed also raises $r_L$, so the negative weight on $r_H$ past $w_H\approx0.81$ is not the cause. Take the best first seeds at $\lambda=2$: the $H$ seed raises $r_H$ by $0.060$ and $r_L$ by $0.011$, the $L$ seed by $0.022$ and $0.018$. The $H$ seed's gain in $F_\lambda$ stays positive until $\lambda\approx1.05$ ($-0.046$ at $\lambda=2$), but the $L$ seed scores higher once $\lambda>0.67$. This fits $6$ of $10$ seeds in $L$ at $\lambda=0.6$ and no clear trend past $\lambda=1$. The best seeds may differ at other $\lambda$, so these thresholds are approximate.
\item \emph{All-$L$ at $\lambda=2$ comes from the objective.} Forcing $0$ to $10$ seeds into $L$, $F_\lambda$ on the training realizations rises with each added $L$ seed, from $-0.27$ to $0.15$, so greedy's order is an unlikely cause, though each split is still built greedily. Excluding seeds from the rates still gives $10$ of $10$.
\item \emph{The ceiling model does not fit this setup.} Claim 1's split puts $4$ of $10$ seeds in $L$ and leaves a gap of $0.26$ (PoF $1.10$). The same first seeds show why. The $H$ seed converts about $11.0$ nodes of $H$ and $0.47$ of $L$; the $L$ seed converts $0.74$ of $L$, counting itself, and $4.0$ of $H$. Within-group yields thus differ about $15$-fold, against $a_H/a_L\approx2.9$, which fits direction 1's compounding hypothesis ($\beta_H/\beta_L>a_H/a_L$). But the cross-group yields are large, so a model with a yield for each pair of groups is needed. A single $b$ cannot fit both groups: calibrated on $H$ ($b\approx13.6$) it gives $k^*\approx8<10$, past Claim 1's range; calibrated on $L$ ($b\approx2.6$) it gives $k^*\approx40$, where Claim 1 predicts DP $=$ maximin, yet strict DP pays PoF $2.47$ against worst-first's $1.35$ at the same worst rate (Section~\ref{sec:compare}).
\item \emph{Seeding $L$ does not persuade more of $L$.} $L$'s non-seed rate falls from $0.075$ with no $L$ seeds to $0.051$ with $10$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Limitations.} These tests are preliminary. They use one small synthetic network of $500$ nodes and do not cover large or real graphs, other homophily levels, or other settings of $\sigma$, $p$ and the rumor seeds. We report no error bars, and DP greedy's PoF is not even monotone in $\mu_L$ ($2.47$ at $0.3$, $2.40$ at $0.15$). PoF is measured against total greedy, a heuristic, not against the optimum. We varied $\lambda$ only at $k=10$, $\mu_L=0.3$; we expect looser values to soften the cost in A and B as well, but have not tested this.

\textbf{Takeaway.} Under unequal acceptance, what is measured decides the conclusion. Parity on saved nodes is cheap. Strict parity on converted nodes is costly and is reached mainly by holding back the receptive group, while looser penalties trade total for gap gradually.
